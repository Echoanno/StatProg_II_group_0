### Xiaohan Liu:

#### Topic

Changes in household structure in Munich over the past 16 years. 

##### 1. Overall household size trends

How has the distribution of household sizes in Munich changed over the past 16 years?

-   Has there been a clear shift toward 1–2 person households?
-   Are there identifiable turning points in the trend?

##### 2. Ageing and single-person households

To what extent has population ageing contributed to the growth of single-person households?

-   Focus on elderly people (65+) who are widowed or divorced.
-   Examine their contribution to the increase in one-person households.

##### 3. Marriage patterns and household forms

How are changing social values reflected in household structures?

-   Investigate whether growth in unmarried populations (“ledig”) is associated with growth in two-person households.
-   Explore whether this pattern may be consistent with non-marital cohabitation trends.

#### Potential Variables

-   Household size (Personenzahl)
-   Age group (Altersgruppen)
-   Marital status (Familienstand)
-   Year / time period

#### Planned Analyses

-   Descriptive statistics
-   Trend analysis over time
-   Visualisations of household structure changes
-   Relationship analysis between demographic variables

#### Notes / Concerns from other team members

-   The general topic appears feasible and sociologically meaningful, especially because Munich is a useful case study for ageing, urbanisation, changing family structures, and rising single-person households.

-   The proposed structure is strong because it combines:

    -   descriptive analysis,
    -   demographic trend analysis,
    -   and possible sociological interpretation.

-   However, we should be careful not to make overly strong causal or theoretical claims based only on aggregate demographic data.

-   In particular, Research Question 3 may be methodologically difficult:

    -   an increase in unmarried people (“ledig”) together with growth in two-person households does not necessarily prove non-marital cohabitation.
    -   Two-person households may also include roommates, family members, elderly couples, or other living arrangements.

-   The strongest and most feasible part of the project currently appears to be:

    -   the relationship between population ageing and growth in one-person households.

#### New ideas after meeting

The growth of single-person households may be associated with increasing international migration and foreign resident populations in Munich.
or
Foreign population growth may represent one possible contributing factor. 

During the preliminary exploration of the household dataset, Xiaohan Liu observed that the proportion of single-person households in Munich has increased noticeably over time.

At the same time:

the proportions of two-, three-, four-, and five-person households appeared relatively stable,
and marital status distributions (married, single, divorced) also did not show dramatic structural changes.

This led to a discussion about whether the increase in one-person households could be linked to demographic changes other than shifts in marriage patterns.

One possible explanation proposed during the meeting was the growth of Munich’s foreign resident population and migration-related demographic changes. The idea is that international students, young professionals, and newly arrived residents may be more likely to live alone, especially in a large and internationally connected city such as Munich.

The group agreed that this could become a promising exploratory research direction, but that the analysis should be framed carefully as an investigation of demographic associations rather than direct causal proof.
